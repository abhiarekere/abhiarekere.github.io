Chromosome Number Evolution in Carnivora

This project investigated how range size and genetic drift influence chromosome number evolution across Carnivora. I built reproducible R workflows to clean, integrate, and analyze comparative genomics datasets, including chromosome counts, GBIF species occurrence data, and phylogenetic trees, and applied phylogenetic modeling and Bayesian statistical analysis to test whether species with smaller range sizes show elevated rates of chromosome fusions and fissions. This work contributed computational analysis to a peer reviewed publication in the Journal of Heredity.

Clinical Genomics Workflow Monitoring

Industry-Sponsored Biomedical Engineering Senior Design Project

Working with an industry sponsor, our team built a modular lab operations platform to simulate, monitor, and analyze the end-to-end journey of clinical test samples through a high-throughput clinical genomics testing workflow. The goal was to give lab operations real visibility into how samples move through testing — surfacing bottlenecks, rework, and turnaround-time risk before they became problems.

What we built

  • Directed acyclic graph (DAG)-based simulator to generate realistic synthetic sample data with probabilistic state transitions
  • Hybrid PostgreSQL and Amazon Redshift database architecture to store and scale workflow data
  • dbt transformations to turn raw sample transitions into structured, queryable analytics products
  • FastAPI backend serving workflow metrics and sample-level data to a front-end dashboard
  • Dashboard surfacing pipeline status, bottlenecks, rework, SLA risk, and predicted vs. actual turnaround time
  • Regression model to estimate sample turnaround time and flag at-risk samples for proactive intervention

Tools: Python, SQL, PostgreSQL, dbt, Amazon Redshift, FastAPI, Cursor
