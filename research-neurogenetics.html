Summer 2023 & Summer 2025

Neurogenetics Assay Development & Sequencing Data Analysis

Abhimanyu Arekere

Neurogenetics Lab · Texas A&M University

Abstract

Across two summer research experiences in a neurogenetics lab, I worked with model organisms including Mexican cavefish and Drosophila to study neural and behavioral phenotypes. My work included brain dissections, tissue mounting, microscopy, immunofluorescence staining, behavioral assay design, and computational analysis of biological data. I also ran R-based single-nucleus RNA-seq analysis workflows using tools such as Seurat, Signac, and Monocle3, gaining exposure to sequencing-derived neurogenetics datasets and reproducible computational biology workflows.

The Problem

Neurogenetics experiments often require connecting biological structure, neural function, and measurable behavior. This creates two major challenges: first, behavioral assays must be designed carefully so that the measured behavior reflects the intended phenotype rather than a confounding variable; second, experimental outputs such as microscopy images, behavioral results, and sequencing data must be translated into quantitative, interpretable datasets.

In my Drosophila work, one specific challenge was that locomotor behavior could interfere with interpretation of olfactory assay results. If a fly's movement ability affected its response in the assay, then the experiment could mistakenly measure locomotor impairment instead of olfactory deficit. This motivated an assay modification to improve experimental control.

What I Worked On

  • Designed Drosophila behavioral assays to quantify olfactory deficits associated with neurodegeneration-related phenotypes.
  • Developed an agar-based assay modification to reduce locomotor bias and improve the interpretability of behavioral results.
  • Performed immunofluorescence staining and microscopy of Drosophila brain tissue and glial cells.
  • Conducted brain dissections, tissue mounting, ablations, and microscopy in Mexican cavefish neurogenetics experiments.
  • Ran R-based snRNA-seq analysis workflows for larval cavefish datasets using Seurat, Signac, Monocle3, and related packages.
  • Analyzed behavioral, imaging, and experimental assay data using Python, R, Excel, and lab software.

Engineering Angle

This work strengthened my ability to approach biological experiments as systems that can be measured, modified, and improved. The Drosophila assay modification followed an engineering-style workflow: identify a source of bias, redesign the assay environment, and improve the reliability of the experimental readout. The computational work also exposed me to reproducible biological data processing through R-based sequencing analysis workflows.

Methods & Workflow

The experimental side of this work involved preparing biological samples, performing dissections, mounting tissue, conducting microscopy, and running behavioral assays. The computational side involved running established R workflows for neurogenetics sequencing datasets, managing packages, executing scripts, troubleshooting errors, and interpreting outputs from single-cell and single-nucleus analysis tools.

Together, these experiences connected wet-lab experimentation with quantitative analysis, helping me understand how experimental design, data quality, and computational workflows influence the reliability of biological research conclusions.
